Giving the form definition — three ways
  • A · Tinted wells. The step keeps a white body, but every input sits in a warm well (`#f7f2ea`) with a hairline. Definition comes from the fields themselves — you can see where to type without a single new band. Quietest, and it survives a long form best.
  • B · Sage-capped steps. The step header takes the same sage gradient as every card on the reading pane, so the journey stops being the one place in the app without that grammar. Strongest structure; the risk is that with several steps open it starts to look striped.
  • C · Both — sage cap, tinted wells, and a barely-there warm gradient under the body. My pick: the cap says this is a section, the wells say this is where you write, and they do different jobs rather than competing. The gradient is doing about 3% of the work and can go if it reads as dirty on your screen.

Also corrected here
  • Chips appear only once earned. At stage 1 the header carries no chips at all — nothing has been confirmed, so a row of empty rings is just noise where the eye should be going to the question. Each chip appears, ticked, as its phase is completed. Toggle Stage 1 to see the header without them.
  • The selected agent is a band, not a card. It was a 130px hero for a fact you just chose. Name, agency, the open-queries line and Change — one row, and the step below starts ~90px higher.
  • The glance panel is the specced one in both journeys — sage cap with glyph, status pill, two-up stats, italic policy line, ruled rows, footer stamp, dashed rim. Record must use this same chassis with reply-oriented rows; the pink "FOR REFERENCE" block in your screenshot is the old FocusForm styling, which the fix pack replaces.
