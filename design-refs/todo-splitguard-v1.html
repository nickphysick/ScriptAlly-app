Eight seconds, bottom-left, one at a time. Nothing the menu can do is unrecoverable, which is what actually makes a split button safe — the seam guards stop the mis-click, and the receipt makes the ones that get through cost a click rather than a query.
Why this holds up. The mis-click risk I flagged was the caret sitting flush against a high-consequence primary. Three of the four guards work on the seam itself — widened target, arm-before-press, dead gutter, commit-on-release. The fourth works on the consequence: neither half of the split does anything final. The primary opens a recording flow you can cancel; the menu's destructive verbs are buried below a dead zone and write an undoable receipt.

Keyboard. Tab reaches the split as one stop; Enter fires the primary, ↓ or ALT+↓ opens the menu — the standard split-button contract, so the caret is never the only route in. Snooze presets keep number keys, so the verb that lost its button did not lose its speed.

Column cost. 118px against 216px for the current four-slot grid. That is nearly 100px back to the title and pill columns on every row.
