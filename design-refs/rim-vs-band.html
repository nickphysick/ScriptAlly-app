The mechanism

  • An inset box-shadow paints on the element's own background layer — below every child.
  • The sage band is a child with an opaque background running to the container's top edge.
  • So the band covers the rim's top edge. The sides survive because nothing reaches them.
  • overflow:hidden clips the band's corners to the radius, which is why the failure reads as a clean cut rather than a fade — and why it looks like a clipping bug when it is a paint-order bug.

Which fix

  • B is my recommendation. One rule, on the container, above everything. It cannot be defeated by any child, present or future, and it needs no coordination between two selectors. The rim also stays a single source of truth for hover.
  • C works but is fragile — the band's hairlines and the container's rim must be changed together forever. This is the duplicate-rule trap: when someone retunes the rim token, one of the two will be missed.
  • D is the existing MountCard law and is correct, but it is a structural change to every container and only necessary when a child fill must be physically clipped. Here the rim only needs to be painted last.

Standing rule this should become

Where a container has a child whose background meets its edge — a header band, a coloured footer, a full-bleed image — the rim must be a ::after ring overlay, not an inset shadow on the container and not a border. Inset shadows sit under children; borders resample under transforms. Only an overlay ring survives both. Assert it: a test that the container's rim is declared on a pseudo-element wherever a header band is present.
