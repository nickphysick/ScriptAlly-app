The grammar
  • Something happened → Record response. One journey, one entry point, same shape as Logging new query — same stagger, same step stack, same three exits. "Mark closed" folds in as an outcome; the inline "What happened next?" panel goes, because two doors into one room is what made the all-queried block confusing.
  • I got a fact wrong → correct that entry. Attached to the entry in the timeline, on hover. Never a global Edit — a toolbar button cannot know which of five recorded events you meant.
  • Which means Edit does not survive as a toolbar verb. Every real use of it is one of the three: record a response, correct an entry, or change a record detail in place (materials, sent date, agent) where "What you sent" already does this with MARK SENT and + ADD.

How the fork got answered
  • The original question was "did something change, or am I fixing a mistake?" — but asking it as a modal makes the writer classify their own intent before they can act. Where they clicked already answers it. Record response appends; Correct this edits. No fork, no dialogue.
  • Step 1 decides the journey. Requests ask what was wanted and by when; an offer asks the terms and the reply-by date; a rejection asks only whether anything was said worth keeping. The step stack changes with the outcome — try each.
  • A correction leaves a quiet marker. The entry carries a muted "Edited" with the date on hover. Honest without turning a typo fix into an event. Save the correction to see it appear.
  • Deleting is inside the correction editor, not on the row. Removing a recorded event is a correction, and it should require having opened the thing first.

The thing to watch: recomputeQuery derives status from the log, so correcting an old entry can move the query's current status and its pipeline dates. The editor should say what will change before it saves — that is a build question, and worth settling in the prompt rather than in the UI.
