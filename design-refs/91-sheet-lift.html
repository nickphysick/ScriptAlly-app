Why square corners are right here, and why they are right only here
  • Paper is cut, not moulded. Every surface in this app has a radius — cards, panels, chips, the shell. A sheet with square corners is the one thing that reads as a physical page laid on the desk rather than another UI container. The departure is the point: it is the only square-cornered surface in the product, so it can only ever mean "this is the sheet".
  • The edge is two lines, not one. A hairline of ink plus an inner line of white — the way a cut sheet catches light along its edge. One line alone reads as a border; two read as thickness.
  • The scrim is a warm radial, not a flat black. The desk falls away toward the corners and stays lightest behind the sheet, so the light appears to come from above the page. Flat black would say "modal"; this says "the lamp is on the letter".

The three depths
  • Laid on the desk — a close, tight shadow and the faintest scrim. The sheet is resting, not hovering. Most restrained; risks reading as flat at a glance.
  • Lifted — a layered shadow (contact line, mid, and a long ambient) with a moderate scrim. The sheet is a few millimetres off the desk. My pick: unmistakably raised, still furniture rather than theatre.
  • Deep stage — the room goes properly dark and the sheet floats. Genuinely dramatic, and I would keep it in reserve: use it for Offer alone if you want one outcome to feel different, rather than making every reply this heavy. A journey used weekly should not be a stage every time.

What it costs: the sheet is a real overlay, so it needs a focus trap, Esc, scrim-click to cancel, and aria-modal — the shell has none of that today, and this is the change that turns the takeover into a dialogue. Worth naming in the prompt rather than discovering later. Motion is a 420ms lay-down; reduced motion cuts to the final frame with the scrim still applied.
