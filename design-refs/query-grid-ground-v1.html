What the research says. The products that make white cards look intentional all solve the same problem one of two ways. Either the page is not white — Notion, Stripe, Apple, Airtable put cards on a visibly darker ground so the card's own colour is the contrast — or the card has a drawn edge — Linear, Figma, Craft rely on a 1px hairline (or a paper-edge highlight) and keep shadows for hover. Nobody who does it well relies on a soft shadow alone against a near-white sheet, which is what you had, and the well was an attempt to fix that by adding a fourth surface. Removing a surface works better than adding one.

My pick: A, the Notion pattern. Drop the parchment page card so the grid sits on the app ground #f2ede7; the white cards, tiles and toolbar are then the only light surfaces, and the whole page reads as objects on a desk. It's the least work (one surface removed), it needs no new colour, and it makes Contact list and Query Centre agree — Contact list already sits on the ground. E is the same idea with more air and deeper shadows; it's handsome and it costs a fifth of the vertical density. D's paper edge is the most on-brand if you want to keep the parchment page — it reads as stationery — and it's my second choice. B and C are the flat, product-y versions; both clean, both a little cold against Playfair. F outlines each card in its state colour, which is striking but doubles what the band already says.
