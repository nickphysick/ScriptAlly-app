When it's been your move a long time

Waiting is white and breathing. Your move is blush — and it gains weight with duration, in one hue, with no motion, no red, and no word that judges. The hatched stretch is the time beyond when it was expected: not a penalty, just the length of the wait made visible.

Fresh

It became your move in the last few days. Pale blush, quiet. It is on the list; it is not shouting.

Settled

A week or two. Standard blush — the weight the your-move bars have always had.

Long-standing

Deeper blush, heavier text, and the hatched overrun stretching back to the date it was expected. The count is stated as a fact: 41 days.

Why not red, and why not the word. Red is reserved in this app, and lateness isn't a rejection. "Overdue" is a verdict — it says someone failed. Neither is true: an agent hasn't broken a promise by being slow, and a writer hasn't failed by not having sent the full yet. What is true is the duration, so that is what's drawn and what's counted.

Why weight rather than motion. The waiting bars already breathe. If long-standing work also moved, the two would compete and the page would read as agitated. Weight is loud in a still frame and silent in a moving one — which is exactly the right way round.

The other half of urgency is sort, not colour. "Longest waiting" lifts these to the top of the board. A bar that is both heaviest and first is unmissable without raising its voice.
