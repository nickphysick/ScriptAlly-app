Birds-eye as a world you explore

It opens with your whole campaign on one screen, every query against the one finish line. Pinch, or scroll with the trackpad, to zoom in, and drag in any direction to move around. As you get closer, more appears: names, then the figures, then each query's dates and stages. Labels stay sharp and readable at every zoom, the way a map's do. Rows open up faster than the time across, so the finish line is never far away. On a computer, use the + and − buttons, double-click, or hold Ctrl and scroll.

How it behaves

  • Fits first. It always opens zoomed out so everything fits, and "See it all" brings you back from wherever you are.
  • Detail by distance, not by tapping. What a row shows depends on how big it is on screen (see the levels on the left), so you never get tiny unreadable text or giant blurry text.
  • The header gets out of the way. It's one compact navy band with the four numbers as chips. Zoom in and it folds to a single line, keeping any filter you've chosen as a small chip, so the world gets the screen.
  • Labels stay on screen. Names and group headings slide along the left edge, and the finish line's label stays pinned under the header.
  • The mini map appears once you've zoomed in, showing which part of the world you're looking at.
  • Tap still works. A tap without a drag opens the query's story. A double tap zooms in on that spot. Moves glide to a stop, and nothing animates with reduced motion.
  • Every stage on its own dates. Each bar runs end to end through the stages the query has been through: queried, partial asked, partial sent and so on, each in its Plumage colour, on its real dates. Past its date is hatched, and the time still to come is a dashed outline. Line the rows up on each query's reply date to compare them, or switch to a calendar with today as the line.
  • Why the finish line suits zooming. Zoomed out, it's a picture: the blush area past the line, and which groups are long. Zoomed in, it's a list you can act on. A calendar would need sideways scrolling just to understand it. This world doesn't.
