1 · WHERE ART GOES — AND WHERE IT DOESN’T — three candidate homes, two rejected with reasons.
In the page header NO
DECORATIVE BANNERArt beside every title

A permanent illustration in the header is seen five hundred times and read once. It pushes the work down the page on every visit, and your headers already carry meaning — Playfair title, derived subtitle, the tool row. Decoration that never changes becomes furniture, then becomes noise.

Beside the cards NO
PER-CARD ICONOGRAPHYA little drawing per kind

Kind is already carried by the pastille band and the family colour. A second signal in the same place doesn’t reinforce, it competes — and sixteen small illustrations on one screen is a sticker album, not a desk.

In the empty and the earned YES
EMPTY & MILESTONE STATESWhere the interface has nothing to show, or something to celebrate

Illustration earns its place exactly where data doesn’t exist yet, or where a moment deserves marking. It costs nothing when the page is busy, appears when the page would otherwise be blank, and is seen rarely enough to stay charming.

The principle: art goes where the interface has nothing to say (an empty column, a fresh page, a cleared desk) or something to mark (a flow completed, a week reviewed). Never in the chrome, never repeated per item, never between the user and their work. That keeps the illustrator’s budget on six pieces that land rather than forty that decorate — and every one of them is a moment the writer will actually feel.
