How packages work

Three stages, and the few rules worth knowing before you build one.

Stage one

Add your materials

Versions of your covering letter, synopsis and sample pages — written here or pasted in. Most writers keep two or three of each, so they can test which one lands.

Stage two

Bundle them into a package

Pick one of each and name the combination. Only the covering letter is required — send what each agency actually asks for.

Stage three

Track what comes back

Attach a package when you log a query. Replies land against it, and each material's requests are counted across every package that carries it.

Worth knowing
A sent package stops changing

Once a package has gone out with a query, its contents are fixed. That's what keeps tracking honest — otherwise editing it would rewrite what agents received. Need a different combination? Duplicate & edit makes a new one.

A query takes a package or loose materials

Not both. Attach a package and its contents go exactly as they are, uneditable from the query. Or list materials individually on that query alone — useful when an agent asks for something unusual.

Counts, never scores

Tracking reports what happened — six sent, two replied, one request. It doesn't rank your materials or tell you which is best. Small numbers rarely mean much; you decide when they do.
