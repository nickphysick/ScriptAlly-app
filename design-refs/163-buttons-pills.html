The bar. Every control becomes a real button with a hairline outline; hover darkens the outline rather than adding a ground. A keeps the pink filled primary as the only ground on the page, which preserves the rule that has held all along — one filled control, and it is the one that creates something. B gives Record response and Nudge a burgundy outline if the two query verbs should outrank the three closing ones by more than text weight. ⚠️ A is my pick: with every button now outlined, weight and position already separate the groups, and burgundy on two of seven starts the bar competing with the header's status mark.

Materials. The sample names itself once saved — First 5,500 words, First 3 chapters, First 50 pages — which is both shorter and more informative than Opening sample · 3 ch, and removes the need for a separate size chip. ⚠️ The label is derived from unit and quantity, never stored, or it will drift the first time someone edits the number. Other shows its own free text, which is the same principle.
On the colour: pink fill reads strongest and is what you asked for; the pink outline gets most of the prominence while keeping filled pink reserved for actions. ⚠️ Worth knowing that with the bar's primary, the selected row, and now the materials, pink would be doing three jobs on one screen — the outline keeps the materials legible as objects rather than buttons, since a filled pink pill reads as clickable in a bar that also uses filled pink for its primary action.

The composer. The jump to two rows is a measuring fault: the auto-grow reads scrollHeight while padding is still in the box, so the first keystroke computes a height one line taller than the content. Setting height to auto before measuring, with an explicit line-height and border-box, holds it at one row until the text genuinely wraps. Save is always present, faded and inert with nothing to send, lit as soon as there is.
