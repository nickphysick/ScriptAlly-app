All three share the fixed chassis: square card with the sage top edge (the header's own treatment — this is a serious surface), Playfair question, mono subject line, stacked actions each carrying its own cost, Cancel quiet at the foot. The dependant warning strip slots between header and body when one exists.

A · The ledger is the most scannable: a label column in mono, then was → becomes with the old value struck and the new in ink — and the status row renders the real StatusDot rings, so the change reads in the app's own vocabulary. Scales to many rows without getting louder.
B · The timeline previews itself. The removed rungs struck and faded, the connector dashed through them, the survivors solid — the answer to "what will this do" shown as the thing it will do it to, with one summary line beneath for the non-timeline effects. Most honest, and nothing to learn; it costs height, and a change with many non-timeline effects leans on the summary line.
C · Prose is quietest and reads like the product speaks elsewhere, but consequences buried mid-sentence are easy to skim past, and it degrades worst as effects multiply.

⚠️ My pick: B, with A as its fallback. The timeline preview when the change touches the timeline — which is nearly always, and it is literally the same renderer showing a proposed log, so it can never lie. The ledger for the rare change whose effects are mostly elsewhere. Both share the chassis, so the fallback is a body swap, not a second design.
