What else best-in-class grids do

Five things the products that do this well all have, applied to your page. Register chip locked, Notion styling, no well.

1 · Attention sort — the default order is "what needs me"

Things 3's Today, Superhuman's split inbox, Linear's triage. The most valuable thing a tracker can do is put the cards that want you at the top without being asked. A new Sort key, Attention, orders by register — your move, then past expected, then offers, then calm by nearest expected date, then closed — and becomes the Grid's default. Sort by anything else remains one click away. The register you just locked is exactly the derivation this needs; nothing new is stored.

2 · Verbs on hover — act from the card

Linear, Gmail, Notion. Hovering a card fades its fact line and reveals the same verb row the list has — primary, snooze, close, ⋯ — with keyboard hints. The desk opens from the card exactly as it does from the list row. Nobody has to open the drawer to nudge someone. Hover the cards below.

3 · Saved views — the tiles grown into tabs

Notion, Airtable, Linear. A view is a filter + group + sort + layout you've named. Ship four: Needs me · Waiting · This fortnight (anything with an expected date inside two weeks) · Closed, plus + Save view for the writer's own. The stat tiles keep their counts; the tabs carry the intent. This is what makes the page open on the right thing every morning.

5 · Keyboard — move and act without the mouse

Linear, Superhuman, Gmail. J/K move focus between cards (a sage ring), Enter opens the drawer, R Record response, N Nudge, S Snooze, X Close, 1–4 switch views, / search (already), ? shows the sheet. The hints appear on the hover verbs, so they teach themselves.

What I'd do, in order.

  1. Attention sort as the Grid default. One derivation you already have, one new sort key, and the page starts opening on the right cards. Smallest change, largest effect.
  2. Hover verbs on the card. The desk, the predicate and the popovers all exist; this is a third anchor for them.
  3. Keyboard. Cheap once 2 exists — the hints ride on the verbs.
  4. Empty states. Two cards, one afternoon, and they're the first thing a new writer sees.
  5. Saved views. The biggest and the only one that adds data (a saved view is a stored thing). Worth it, but last, and worth its own design pass on where the tabs sit relative to the tiles.

Things I looked at and would not do: bulk select (nudge-all is the only safe bulk verb and it's rare); drag to reorder or between columns (status is derived); card size slider for the grid (List and Board already cover density); inline editing on the card (the desk is the door, and the record needs its preview).
