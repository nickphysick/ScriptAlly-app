The contradiction has one cause: nothing distinguishes whose window it is. A response window can arrive from three places, and the display treats them as one fact:
SourceWhose factWhat it may say
Agent record's stated timeThe agency's claimPriya advises 6 weeks
Date the writer setYour own estimateyou expect a reply by 12 June
House 8/12/12-week defaultNobody'sNothing at all
Card 2 is the case that produced your screenshot: a date you entered, rendered as though Priya had said it, while the body line correctly reported she had not. Both sentences were true; the attribution was invented. ⚠️ The last pack removed the house assumption from every reader — this is one level up, so the sweep could not have caught it.

The chip is gone in all four. Its job was to give a claim its own surface; attribution in the line does that better and costs no space. The expiry date stays on the bar's end label — one fact per surface, and card 2's label says Your estimate so the bar cannot be misread either.

Setting a date uses the agent profile's own response-time control, in place: eyebrow, slider, the resolved date shown live beneath. ⚠️ The eyebrow reads Your expected response time deliberately — the same words that keep the estimate yours in the display should be the words on the control that sets it. It writes to the query, never to the agent record.
