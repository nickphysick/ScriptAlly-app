Making more of the date

A calendar leaf: a tear-off page with the month in the card's own band tint, the day in Playfair, and two binder pins. Four placements.

My pick: A. One leaf, right of the name block, carrying the date that defines the card — sent for anything with the agent, requested for anything with you, received for offers, closed for closed. The month strip picks up the band tint so the leaf belongs to its card, and the fact line loses the date and gains a little room: "Reply expected by 23 Sept · 3 weeks waiting". It's a real object on the card without competing with the chip.

B is the most informative — sent and expected as two leaves with an arrow — but it puts the calendar on the wrong side of the rule, crowds the materials cluster, and the expected date is a derivation, not an event; giving it a leaf makes it look recorded. C is quiet and cheap and would do if you find the leaf too illustrative. D is the boldest — the leaf becomes the card's mark and the monogram shrinks into the band — but that demotes the agent, and the agent is who the card is about.
