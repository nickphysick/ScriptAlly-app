The calendar — Porcelain, settled

Scrawls trail their own row’s last piece. Controls are cut-corner buttons — bars are 999px pills, controls are square-cornered, so a control can never again be mistaken for data. The named-date notches are gone: the fill already states that a date was named, and the bar ends on it. Their captions moved onto the bars, which now carry a hover tooltip — so the date survives at long ranges where labels drop out.

The fill rule keeps the data honest: a bar fills only when it has a named end — a stated response time, a promised send date, your own reminder. The fill is simply elapsed time over the stated span, derived at read, nothing stored. A bar with no named date stays unfilled — an open-ended wait, and the emptiness says so. A finished stretch is full. A bar at ≥85% deepens one step, which retires the pulse animation entirely. Past a named date the bar caps at full and continues hollow — lateness is drawn, never named. Does it earn its place? The audit

KEEPThe fill. It answers the question a writer actually opens the calendar with — how far through the wait am I? — without arithmetic. Day-counts state the past; the fill shows position within the stated span. And it carries a second fact for free: a filling bar means someone named a date, an unfilled bar means nobody did. It also replaces the pulse (motion) with position (state), which is calmer and reduced-motion-proof.
KEEPAction column, hover captions, ! and clock markers, notches, hollow continuation, group sentences. Each states a fact or offers exactly one deed; none decorates.
EARN ITScrawls. Where a scrawl adds a fact the row doesn't already show — Send it · due 15 days ago carries the date — it's the best thing on the board. Where it triples what the button and bar already say — Nudge them · due today beside a NUDGE button and a bar reading "Nudge due" — it's charm doing no work. Proposed rule: a scrawl must contain a fact absent from its own row, or it doesn't render. On this demo board that keeps three of five.
EARN ITCut by manuscript. Genuinely useful from the second manuscript onward; noise before that. Proposed: the control only renders when more than one manuscript exists.
CUTThe named-date notch. It once distinguished someone named this date from this is merely where the bar stops. The fill now carries that distinction on its own — a filling bar means a date exists, an empty one means nobody set it — and the bar terminates on the date regardless. Three statements of one fact. Removed, with its caption moved to the bar’s hover so nothing is lost at long ranges.
CUTThe 1-week range. On a board of relationships measured in weeks, seven days shows fragments of bars and answers nothing the To-do list doesn't answer better. Range becomes 1 · 3 · 6 months.
CUTThe elapsed wash (R6). Honest reversal of my own proposal: once bars fill, the fill already encodes elapsed time — a second elapsed treatment on the canvas would say the same thing twice. R6 belonged to the flat-bar world.
