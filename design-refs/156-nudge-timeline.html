The nudge record, and how closure gets offered without nagging
  • Each nudge is a minor event — small hollow marker, one line, no title — so a follow-up never claims the weight of a request. Its wording states the outcome, not the act: Nudged — no reply, and once something comes back, simply Nudged, because the reply below it says the rest.
  • The waiting event carries a nudge history line: Nudged twice · 19 Mar 25, 2 Jun 25. This is the single most useful fact in a long silence and it currently exists nowhere — without it a writer scrolls the timeline to work out whether they already followed up.
  • Closure is offered once, and only when the facts justify it. In the first card there is no offer at all — one nudge and four months is an ordinary state of affairs. In the second, three facts have accumulated — two nudges, no reply, fourteen months past the window — so the offer appears, states exactly those three facts, and gives both answers equal standing: Mark closed and Keep tracking.
  • It never repeats. Choosing Keep tracking dismisses it for that query; it does not return next month with the same suggestion in stronger words. A prompt that reappears is nagging, and this product's whole premise is that it reports and the writer decides.
  • The threshold should be facts, not time. My proposal: the offer appears once at least one nudge has gone unanswered and the window closed more than six months ago — so it is triggered by the writer having already tried, not merely by the clock. A query never nudged never gets the offer, because the obvious next step is a nudge, not closure.
  • The third card is the case worth designing for — the nudge worked. The reply becomes an incoming event, the waiting state restarts from what the agent actually said, and the bar goes back to open and green. Nothing about the earlier silence lingers; the record keeps it, the state moves on.
