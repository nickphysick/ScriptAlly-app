Undo
The three exits are three different statements
  • Cancel / Esc — nothing happened. 150ms, single gesture, straight out. No receipt, no row, no trace. Deliberately faster than the entrance: undoing an opening should not feel like an event. Never reverse the stagger — a staggered exit makes leaving feel like work, and the writer who opened this by accident has to sit through it.
  • Save query — the form becomes the row. The takeover lifts and fades upward while the new row lands at the top of the list beneath it, carrying a brief sage ring that settles into the normal rim. The eye follows one object turning into another, which is the whole claim the motion is making: what you filled in is now that line. A receipt confirms it with an Undo.
  • Save & log another — you did not leave. The takeover does not exit at all. The body wipes and reseats, the chips reset, focus returns to the search field, and a session tally appears in the header. The new row still lands in the list behind, so nothing is lost when you eventually close. Exiting and re-entering here would be a lie: you never went anywhere.

Things that decide whether this feels right or broken
  • Never play the exit before the write resolves. Animate on success, not on click, or a failed save shows a row that then vanishes. If the write is slow, the button holds and the takeover stays put — a spinner in place is honest; an optimistic exit is not.
  • Focus after every exit. Cancel and Save return focus to the Log query button. Save & log another returns it to the search field, once motion ends.
  • The receipt owns the undo. One receipt at a time; logging another replaces the previous receipt rather than stacking, and the tally does the counting.
  • prefers-reduced-motion cuts every one of these to the final frame — the row simply appears, the takeover simply goes.
