What does a good comp do?Field notes
Slot · 120×64

Places it on a shelf

Comps tell an agent exactly which section of the bookshop your manuscript belongs in.

Slot · 120×64

Names the readership

They point to a set of readers who already buy books like yours.

Slot · 120×64

Backs the sales case

Editors build a book's numbers partly on how its nearest neighbours actually sold.

Slot · 120×64

Carries the tone

A well-chosen pair conveys how the book feels faster than any synopsis can.

Slot · 120×64

Shows you read now

Recent comps — broadly the last three to five years — show you know today's market, not the one you grew up reading.

Slot · 120×64

Comping the giants

Global phenomena can't anchor a realistic case for a debut — and everyone names them.

Slot · 120×64

Reaching too far back

A comp from another publishing era says the market has moved on without you.

Slot · 120×64

Comping the unread

Agents ask about comps. A title you haven't read is easily exposed.

Slot · 120×64

Crossing shelves

Comps from the wrong category muddy where the book sits — and who it's for.

Slot · 120×64

Piling them on

Two is the common counsel, three at most. A longer list dilutes the signal.
