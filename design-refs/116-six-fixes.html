The six
  1. Hairline under the band. White on warm cream is too small a step to divide anything on its own — the band was floating. One hairline and it becomes an edge.
  2. Card header glyphs lose their plates. A glyph on a tinted plate on a coloured band is three surfaces to say one word. The bare mark in the header's own darker sage reads better and stops the header looking like a toolbar.
  3. The agent's initials go soft pink with black text. It is now the only pink surface on the page, which is what makes it work — the current subject is the one thing worth tinting.
  4. Timeline rhythm. Markers up to 27px, one consistent 22px gap between events, the connector running from marker to marker rather than floating, and the title row given the marker's height so text and mark sit on one line. The old version had events at four different spacings depending on whether they carried a body.
  5. Status mark doubled and moved left — 44px, ahead of the words, so the eye meets the symbol and then reads the label. Date sits under the label.
  6. Sage headers return. With the page now unified in neutrals, sage is doing what it should — marking the three cards as one family against a quiet ground. It stopped working before because it was competing with four other tints; it is the only hue on the page now.
