A realistic week — mostly nothing happening

Reply windows run for months, so in any given week most rows have no boundary in them at all. Eleven full-width stripes carry no information. Here quiet windows are rails, and full height is reserved for rows where something actually happens.
What changed, and why. A rail is 12px and carries its label at the end it points to — near the future, where the information is. A bar is 34px and earns that height by containing an event: a node, a handover, a hatched overrun. Scanning the board, your eye now finds the three rows that matter without reading a word.

Geometry from tokens. Every element centres on --row-h rather than a tuned offset, so a row height change can't leave the bars floating — the fault in the shipped build, and the same class as --tl-head-h before it.

One bar, one count. Ellery's overrun is the left portion of a single bar, not a second object beside it, and the duration is stated once.
