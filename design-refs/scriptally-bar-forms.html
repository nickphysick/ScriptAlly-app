Progress bars — five forms

All in ink; each works with whichever fill colour you pick. The thing driving these: your sample sizes are three or four sends. A smooth 75% bar claims a precision that four data points don't have — so the better forms show the denominator, the benchmark, or both.

1 · Tally segments

One block per query sent★ Recommended
Each block is one agent you sent to; filled means they replied. You read the rate and the sample size in the same glyph — four blocks obviously isn't a verdict, twelve obviously is. The gold block is the send that earned a full request, so you can see exactly which one it was. Above about twelve sends it collapses to a plain proportional bar.

2 · Bullet with benchmark

Bar + community median tick
The bar, plus a burgundy tick marking the community median. A bar alone answers "how much"; this answers "how does mine compare" — which is the actual question, and it's the form the research keeps pointing at for exactly this job. It also puts the community data to work visually rather than only in a pill.

3 · Dot plot

Position on a hairline axis
A dot at the value on a dotted axis, with a ghost dot for the community median. The least ink of any option and the most editorial — it belongs to the same family as your two-tone section rules. Best-in-class for small datasets precisely because it doesn't dress four data points up as a solid mass. Trade-off: it reads as a chart, so it needs the axis explained once.

4 · Composition

Replied · still waiting · no reply
One track split three ways. This is the most honest about outstanding queries: an agent who hasn't answered yet isn't a rejection, and a single percentage quietly buries that. Useful when several queries are still live — which, for most writers, is most of the time. Costs a legend, and comparison across lanes is slightly slower.

5 · Tally + benchmark

Forms 1 and 2 combined
Segments for the sends, with a hairline axis beneath carrying the community median tick. You get the sample size, the outcome of each individual send, the request, and the benchmark — in one row, with no legend. Denser than the others; earns its keep on the Analytics tab, likely too much for a card back.

Where I'd land

Tally segments (1) as the house form, used on card backs, the leaderboard and the materials table. It's the only one that makes the small sample self-evident rather than something a caption has to apologise for — and the gold block for the send that won a request is a detail nobody else's tracker has.

Bullet or the hybrid (2 / 5) on the Analytics tab only, once community data is live — that's where the comparison question is actually being asked, and where a benchmark tick has room to be explained. Until the pipeline exists, the tick simply doesn't render, and form 1 stands alone.

Composition (4) for the funnel, where "still waiting" genuinely is its own state and the legend is already justified. And whichever you choose, ink stays the fill — none of these need colour to work, which is the point.
