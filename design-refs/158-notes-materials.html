Materials — your rule, applied. A pill exists only if that material went with the send. Remove it and it disappears; there is no greyed ghost, because a ghost is the app remembering something that did not happen. The way back is + Attach, whose menu lists the complete set of four with the attached ones shown inert — so the menu, not the row, is where you learn what a send could carry. Removal is undoable from the toast for a few seconds, which is what makes an instant disappearance safe. Clicking a pill opens its editor: quantity and unit for the opening sample, free text for Other, and remove at the foot.

Notes. All three move the composer to the top, which is the significant change: a note gets written when you have just read something, so the writing surface should be where the eye already is rather than below a growing list. Newest first for the same reason. The composer is white with a rim, grows with the text, and takes ⌘+Enter — the field currently tints pink and clips its send button.
A keeps notes as tinted cards with day separators and hover-revealed Edit and Delete; the pinned note is pink, which is the one use of pink here. B sets the note body in Caveat — the product's own handwriting voice, already in the design system for exactly this. Charming, and it will fight long or pasted notes. C drops the bubble entirely: notes as ruled entries in a document, which is the most restful and the most in keeping with a card that sits beside a timeline. It also shows the empty state.

My pick: C, with A's day separators — and pinning worth adding whichever you choose, since the one note a writer needs at the top is usually the one about how to reach them.
