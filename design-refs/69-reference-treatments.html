What the research says

Two established patterns bear directly on this, and they agree.

  • Carbon's read-only pattern — the field background blends into the layer beneath, borders lose emphasis to make information more readable, signifier icons take a disabled colour, and text colour does not change so contrast holds. Structure and spacing stay identical to the interactive version. Read-only is explicitly not disabled: it stays keyboard-navigable, because the point is that it gets read.
  • Material 3's tonal separation — surfaces separate by tonal difference rather than by borders, and overlapping containment areas take different surface roles. Elevation rises with tonal prominence, which inverts cleanly for reference material: it should sit lower, not higher.

Both point the same way: a reference surface recedes and drops its affordance signals — it does not take a new hue. Hue changes usually mean semantics elsewhere in a system (error, success, Pro), so borrowing one for "reference" spends a signal you may want later.

How they land here

  • A · Recede is the textbook answer. The panel sits at page-ground tone while the step sits on white, so the tonal gap does the work. Cheapest to build, uses only existing tokens, no new semantics. Risk: at a glance it can read as disabled rather than reference — Carbon's own warning — which is why the mono captions and the caption bar matter.
  • B · Cool tint separates hardest and is the clearest at speed. It costs a hue, collides with Pro slate at #6A89A7, and needs theme-scoping so it can't leak into Bold Pastille or Editorial.
  • C · Marginalia is the one most native to this product. A dashed rim reads as annotation rather than as a second application, and it borrows nothing from anyone else's semantics. It is also the most likely to look decorative rather than deliberate if the dash weight is wrong.
  • D · Well says "not a card" most literally — carved in rather than sitting on. It reads as a container for consultation, and the inner shadow is a treatment used nowhere else in the app, so it carries no existing meaning. Watch it does not read as a disabled input at small sizes.

My recommendation

C, with A's tonal discipline underneath it. Marginalia is the pattern ScriptAlly is already writing in — parchment, mono captions, Playfair italic for the wish list — and it says "consult this" without spending a hue or borrowing a convention from a system this app is not part of. If it reads too light in the browser, D is the fallback: same argument, more structure. B is the one I would hold back, not because it looks wrong — it is the strongest at a glance — but because it makes slate mean two things and adds a theming obligation to every future panel.
