The rows. The problem is that with agent for is printed on every row, and the label is what forces the two-line block that squeezes the dot to 17px. But the group heading already says No reply yet, so the context is established once at the top and repeated ten times below it.
B moves the label into the group head and leaves the row a bare figure — dot back to 26px, one word of furniture instead of ten. C puts the elapsed on the agency line as a mono suffix, freeing the whole right column for a 30px dot; tightest of the four, and the agency name truncates sooner. D replaces the initials with the status mark, which is the boldest: the dot lands where the eye starts and gets full size, at the cost of the avatars that currently distinguish two Marcus Reeds. My pick: C — the elapsed is a property of the query, so it belongs on the query's own line, and it buys the biggest dot of the three that keep initials.

The waiting state. One shape across all three, differing only in what is true.
  • What the agency said is a quoted chip, not body text — Greenfield Literary say eight weeks. It is their claim, and giving it its own surface distinguishes it from the app's own words. The bar and its end labels then measure against that claim rather than an assumption.
  • State 2 adds the closing date to the same chip and the convention line beneath, and the bar goes to spent hatch. The title changes from Waiting to hear back to No reply, which is the only wording change between them.
  • State 3 draws no bar at all — there is nothing to measure. It states the fact plainly and offers the one thing that would change it. ⚠️ Note the copy: do not state a response time, not no expected date set, because the absence is the agency's, not the writer's oversight.
  • The marker carries the difference too: sage ring while the window is open, oat ring once it has passed — so the state is legible from the rail alone.
