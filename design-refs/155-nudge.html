What a nudge leaves behind

Nudged — no reply19 MAR 2025
Availability follows whose turn it is, not whether a date has passed. That is almost certainly the bug: if the current condition is gated on the expected-reply date, most queries have no expected date at all, so it never becomes true and the button is permanently grey.

Disabled states explain themselves. A greyed control that never says why is the weakest thing in a toolbar — worse than no control, because the writer cannot tell whether it is broken or waiting. Both disabled cases name the reason and, where there is one, the thing outstanding: he asked for the first fifty pages on 18 June. The closed case offers the way out instead.

The confirm states facts and stops. What the agency said, when the window closes, how long that is — then Nudge anyway and Cancel. No are you sure, no this may annoy them, no recommendation. The little window bar is the same spent-hatch device as the timeline, filled while the window is open and hatched once it has passed, so the two places that talk about a window look the same.

Two decisions for you. The confirm's primary is pink — the only other pink on the page is Log new query, and the two never appear together, so it is available. And the confirm takes the header's square-with-sage-top treatment; if that feels heavy for a two-line dialog, the alternative is a plain rounded popover.

A nudge is a minor timeline event — small hollow marker, one line, no title — so it sits in the record without competing with a request. That is treatment A's minor-event rule from the timeline pack.
