What is wrong with it now
The row currently mixes three different things on one line — chips that are records, a bare REMOVE that acts on the last chip without saying so, and an upgrade pitch. Nothing indicates what is clickable, and the destructive action is the most prominent word in the row.

A · Attachment chips. Each material is a chip; hovering reveals a small × on that chip, so remove belongs to the thing it removes rather than floating at the end of the row. One + Attach button opens a menu of the four material types, with already-added ones shown as done rather than hidden — so the menu is a complete statement of what a query can carry. The Pro option sits below a rule as one more thing you could attach, which is a far softer pitch than a line of upgrade copy.

A2 shows the edit case. Clicking a chip opens its own popover: quantity stepper and unit for the opening sample, a Mark as sent action, and remove at the bottom in burgundy. The unit chips make the quantity self-describing — 3 chapters reads on the chip afterwards, so you never need to open it to know. The dashed chip is a material added but not yet sent, which the current design cannot express at all.

B · A four-row list. All four material types always visible, each with its state and one action. Nothing is hidden behind a menu, and the empty ones invite completion — Other · Not included · Add. It costs about 70px more height than A and turns the header into a form, but it is the only version where you can see at a glance what you have not sent.

My read: A, with A2's popover. The chips keep the card a header rather than a form, the state each material is in is legible without opening anything, and adding is one button rather than four rows of affordances. B would be right on a dedicated materials screen; here it competes with Tracking for the eye.
