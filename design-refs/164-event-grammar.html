The messiness has one cause: no shared event grammar. Each type invented its own arrangement, so the reminder ended up as a sentence with a link in the middle and a date carrying a SCHEDULED FOR prefix nothing else uses. The fix is a rule every event obeys:

Row 1 — always title · optional qualifier … meta right. The title is Playfair at one size; the qualifier is the only thing allowed beside it; the meta slot holds a date or a duration, mono, right-aligned, with no prefix words.
Row 2 — always the body block, one consistent gap between its parts, nothing sitting outside it. Chips, bars, figures and lines all live here in that order.

Applied to the manuscript, that is why the title shrinks and takes a book icon: it is body content inside row 2, so it cannot outweigh the row-1 title above it. Genre and word count go — they belong to the manuscript, not to this send, and they are already on the page.

The three reminder options all obey the grammar. A makes it a proper event with a faint title and the date in the normal meta slot — most consistent, least special-cased. B puts the scheduled date in the same chip device the agency's claim uses, which reads well but gives a pending thing the same surface as a stated fact. C stops pretending it is an event and makes it a dashed pending panel — clearest signal that it has not happened, but it introduces a fourth shape into a card that just got down to two rows.

⚠️ My pick: A. The dashed ring and faint ink already say "not yet"; the arrangement should stay identical to everything above it, because that consistency is the thing you are missing. SCHEDULED FOR becomes just the date — the tense is carried by the ring, not by a word.
