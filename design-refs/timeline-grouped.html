ScriptAlly · design ref v19

Grouped, and in the writer's words

Two weeks, opening three days back so the recent past gives the bars their footing. Rows are grouped by whose move it is — the page's answer to "what needs me?" before a single row is read. Every sentence is written from the writer's side of the desk; the derivations underneath are unchanged.
Never a pronoun. The app does not know an agent's gender and must not guess: the surname where there is one, “the agent” where there is not. Out with Whitfield, out with the agent — never her or him.

The copy system, in one rule. The label is what the writer would say to a friend: out with Whitfield, they want the full, no word in 40 days, offer on the table. Facts in a human voice — the day-count carries the urgency "overdue" wanted, with none of the verdict. "Reply window" survives only in the derivation's name.

Grouping beats sorting. "Your move" always first with its count; "Out with agents" beneath; "Recently closed" lingers a week then leaves. Sort applies within groups. The three headers are the page's summary.

The past gives the long view its shape. A board that only looks forward is emptiest exactly when zoomed out — six months of forecast holds nothing at rest. Ranges now open a slice back (here 3 of 14 days; a quarter at 3–6 months), so sends, requests and nudges sit on the board as things that happened, and today reads from context, not from a spine.
