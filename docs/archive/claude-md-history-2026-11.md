## Finishing a task — THE PANE READS, THE MODAL FINISHES (⚠️ SUPERSEDED by "Query actions v1" above, 27 Sep — kept as history. task-modal round, 21 Sep; ref `task-modal.html`)

**⚠️ THAT SENTENCE IS THE WHOLE RULE, AND IT IS NICK'S (21 Sep): *"I'd rather hold one sentence than
a flag."*** `TaskPane` is where a writer READS a task — its quick-reference rail, its dated timeline,
its record tiles and its stage ladder are a reading surface and the modal draws none of them.
`TaskModal` is where a task is FINISHED. **Do not reopen the question of whether they duplicate each
other**: they do not, because they answer different questions, and a flag over the top of them would
be two surfaces free to offer different verbs for one card — the fault this page closed once already
when `TodoDock` and `TaskPane` briefly coexisted. Retiring the pane becomes its own pass **once the
`QueryCard` carries the rail, the timeline and the tiles**, which is what it should absorb.

- **⚠️ WORTH A NUDGE = PAST THE REPLY WINDOW, NEVER NUDGED. GONE QUIET = PAST THE WINDOW, ALREADY
  NUDGED, AND PAST THE NEXT NUDGE DATE.** (Nick, 21 Sep.) **The generator agrees** — checked rather
  than assumed: a query 84 days out against a stated 6-week window with **no `lastNudgeSentDate` and
  no `nudgeDate`** raises `nudge_overdue` and lands in *Worth a nudge*; the same query carrying a
  past `lastNudgeSentDate` and a past check-back raises `no_response_close` and lands in *Gone
  quiet*. No disagreement to record.
  - **⚠️ AND THAT IS WHY TWO EARLIER FIXTURES FAILED TO RAISE A NUDGE — they were arguing with the
    rule.** Both carried `lastNudgeSentDate` plus a past `nudgeDate` (84 days / 6-week window, and
    70 days / 16-week window) and both came back closes, which is the product being correct. **The
    absence of those two fields IS the fixture**; adding them describes a different state. Neither
    `noResponseMeansNo`, `scheduledReminder` nor `repliedSinceMs` was deciding it.
- **⚠️ ON THE TO-DO PAGE THE PANE STILL DOES BOTH, AND THAT IS THE STATED INTERIM (Nick, 21 Sep).**
  One asking surface per page holds; it is simply a different one there for now. That page's cards
  open the pane, and the pane's primary routes on `activeFlow.writes` — *the flow declares the
  write, not the card* — which is the modal's own insight already built into a 1,301-line session.
  **Mounting the modal beside it would be two asking surfaces on one card**, the fault the role
  split exists to prevent. Replacing that fork is its own phase, scheduled after the dashboard has
  had a week on dev. **Until then: dashboard finishes in the modal, To-do page finishes in the
  pane, and neither page has two.**
- **⚠️ THE DUPLICATE-SEND GUARD IS BESIDE THE WRITE, NOT BESIDE A SURFACE — and it stopped being
  reached once already without anything going red.** `priorSameTypeSend` was consulted in
  `quickDone` and nowhere else; the dashboard's tick reached it by ROUTE rather than by rule, so the
  moment the tick began committing through `commitSendFromPane` instead, a second full manuscript to
  an agent who already had one was written with no question asked — through a clean production build
  and 8,187 green tests. **The optimistic path was carrying a check nobody had noticed it carried.**
  Both senders consult it now, and `duplicateSendGuard.test.ts` derives the sender list from *who
  calls `recordMaterialsSent`* rather than naming them, so the next sender to arrive is covered by
  construction. A guard in a caller protects that caller and leaves the next one unguarded.
- **⚠️ THE TICK NEVER COMMITS. It opens the modal, and only the modal writes.** Until 21 Sep a tick
  committed on a send and on a nudge with the defaults and asked only on a quiet card. Nick: *"a
  commit the user can't see is a commit they don't trust. One click plus a visible confirmation is
  the price, and it's the right price."* Undo on the row's strip stays as the safety net after.
- **⚠️ EVERY JOURNEY OFFERS THREE ANSWERS, AND A `CommitRequest` KIND NAMES THE WRITE — NEVER THE
  CARD.** `commitFromPane` routes on the CARD's journey, so a send card's values land in the send arm
  however they are filled in; that is what produced a close with no reason when the `nudge` kind was
  added. `close` and `mute` are their own kinds for the same reason. Nick: *"a third time through the
  same seam suggests the seam wants a kind field that names the write, not the card."*
- **⚠️ "Task n of N" MEANS THE LIST YOU CAME FROM, NEVER A GLOBAL.** The dashboard's board is
  manuscript-scoped and capped and the To-do page's `dockable` is neither. **On the dashboard the
  modal's counter may never exceed what the card itself shows** — if the card says "All 18" the modal
  never says "of 19". Each door passes its own ordered list, which makes that true by construction.
- **⚠️ THE FEED'S LINK OPENS THE MODAL FROM THE QUERY, NOT FROM A BOARD ROW, AND COMMITS NOTHING.**
  `markSentOffered` reads the QUERY's status; the board reads task FLAGS. A snoozed or dismissed task
  is suppressed from `live` while the feed still draws the link — so the old `if (r) tick(r)` with no
  `else` **silently did nothing**, on a link that looked live.
- **⚠️ `taskModal.css` DECLARES ITS OWN PALETTE BECAUSE THE MODAL PORTALS TO `document.body`** —
  outside `.os-root`, where every `--dash-*` resolves to nothing and the declaration is dropped in
  silence. `queryCard.css` carries the same block for the same reason.
- **⚠️ A DATE IS TEXT WITH A `Change` LINK, NEVER A BARE `<input type="date">`.** Measured on the
  page: the browser renders its own locale, `02/11/2026`, where every other date in the app reads
  `2 Nov` — so the resting state was the one place the app spoke in a format it uses nowhere else.
- **⚠️ AND THE REF'S `.head h2` IS DECLARED TWICE — the browser takes the second, 24px/1.3, where the
  brief says 25px/1.32.** Measured in the rendered mockup. A reasoned value in prose beats an
  unreasoned one in an artefact; `text-wrap: balance` survived there only by accident.

## The page masthead — ONE FORMAT, ONE BEHAVIOUR (⚠️ SUPERSEDED IN FULL by "The page header" above, 26 Sep — kept as history; the `.wsh` markup, the marks and the settle it describes are gone from live code. 30 Aug; refs `174-pinned-chrome-options.html`, `175-sticky-masthead-chevron.html`, `collapse-transition.html` option B)

**Every masthead behaves identically: it is the first thing in the scroll row, it scrolls away as content, and a 46px collapsed bar takes over.** No sticky slab, no settle, no Hide, no chevron, no fold, no per-page arrangement — and no partition, because a partition needs two behaviours and there is one.

- **⚠️ THE TWO HEADER TYPES ARE DELETED, AND THE LAW THEY REPLACED IS NOT COMING BACK IN A THIRD FORM.** For the record, because everything below is written against them: **Type A · Pinned** had a single primary scroller, pinned masthead-plus-control-row as one sticky slab at `top: 0`, and SETTLED on scroll (mark 52→34, title 30→22, description folded, paddings tightened); **Type B · Static** had none, sat in flow, never settled, and folded to a chevron badge straddling the window's top border via `Hide`. `pinned = !fill || !!settleOn` was the derivation, `data-wpg-type` published it, `.wpg--static` styled the other half, and `mastheadBehaviour.ts` handed it to `PageHeader` so a masthead that LEAVES could refuse an action a pinned one accepted.
- **⚠️ WHAT SURVIVES IS THE DECLARATION OF WHERE A PAGE SCROLLS, RENAMED `scroller` (was `settleOn`; attribute `data-wpg-scroller`).** It is what the BAR watches: a `fill` page's frame never scrolls, so the handoff has to follow the scroll that does. **A prop named for a deleted mechanism is a comment outliving what it described, at the API** — which is why the rename is the point rather than tidiness.
  - **⚠️ AND THE DECLARATION STILL CANNOT BE REPLACED BY DISCOVERY.** "The single element that currently overflows" finds exactly one on Query Centre — one pane happens to hold enough to scroll — and concludes the page has a primary scroller. It has none. Absence is the assertable form of *"no single place this page scrolls"*.
- **⚠️ THE SETTLE'S WHOLE COMPENSATION CHAIN IS DELETED, AND THAT IS THE POINT OF THE REBUILD: a mechanism removed rather than a mechanism made safe.** Settling shed ~62px into `scrollHeight` the instant a page pinned — the SHRINK direction, so a page overflowing by less than the reclaim was clamped to 0, un-settled, and cycled (Noteboard: **37 flips on one downward pass at 47px of overflow**). Three things existed to undo it: a resting height remembered under a `getAnimations({ subtree: true })` guard (a half-settled box captured mid-ease left the next reclaim short — measured, a scroll landing on 4px moved a landmark **59px**), a published `--wpg-reclaim-pad`, and a SPACER holding the gap (padding alone fixed max scroll and let the flow collapse — a 10px wheel tick moved a landmark **67px**). **Nothing changes height on scroll now, so there is nothing to clamp and nothing to compensate.**
  - **THE SPACER SURVIVES AS THE GAP ALONE, AND STILL AS AN ELEMENT.** Adjacent siblings' margins COLLAPSE: as the slab's `margin-bottom` this gap collapsed into the content's own and stopped collapsing the moment the spacer gained height, adding a whole gap to the column on every settle — measured, max scroll 1918 → 1934.
- **⚠️ ANYTHING THAT MUST SIT ON THE WINDOW'S EDGE IS A SIBLING POSITIONED OVER IT, NEVER A CHILD.** `.ws-window` clips (`overflow: hidden`, 16px radius), so the fold's chevron was portalled to `.ws-winwrap`. `shellSlots.ts` existed only to give that portal a host without a leaf component importing the whole shell — which had transitively pulled in Firebase and stopped three unit suites LOADING with `auth/invalid-api-key`. Both are deleted; the constraint is not.
  - **⚠️ AND A PORTAL OUTLIVES ITS PAGE UNDER A DISPLAY-TOGGLING SHELL.** The badge's host was the SHELL's and shared while the state gating it was per-page, so a folded page went on drawing its chevron over whatever page the reader opened next — measured: fold Query Centre, open Comparable titles, QC's chevron floats at y=100 over a page with no fold. **Anything portalled to a shell-level host must also ask whether its page is the one on screen.**
- **⚠️ A CONTROL CANNOT LIVE ON TOP OF AN ILLUSTRATION, AND ON AN ILLUSTRATED BAND THE ONLY FREE EDGE IS A BORDER.** Query Centre's `Hide` was restyled into a chevron pill straddling the masthead's bottom border because the word sat in the densest part of the artwork — pale grey ink over an hourglass, measured and barely readable. Also from that control: **`font-size: 0` removes a label without taking the accessible name with it**; `display: none` on the text, or swapping the element, takes the name too.
- **⚠️ THE WASH IS DELETED (`--mast-wash-top` / `--mast-wash-bottom`), AND SO IS ITS CARVE-OUT.** Every masthead sits on the window's own opaque ground. The carve-out named two pages by hand and held them to a weaker claim, which is the shape that erodes one exemption at a time. **The `background` SHORTHAND rule survives the gradient's removal and is why the ground is a `background-color`:** the shorthand resets every longhand, so a future edit adding an image would silently take the colour with it — this repo has had four rounds of a see-through header from exactly that.
- **⚠️ NOTHING READS THROUGH THE PINNED BAND, AND THAT LOCK MOVED RATHER THAN LAPSING.** The masthead used to pin, so content passed beneath it; the thing content passes beneath is now the collapsed bar. `chromeGround.measure.ts` photographs it over two different backdrops **in the same posture** and requires the images identical — a claim that needs no knowledge of what the band is filled with, which is why it survived the flat fill becoming a gradient and then the gradient's deletion. **Its precondition moved with it: the bar is `opacity: 0` at rest, so both readings must be past the show threshold or the sweep compares two photographs of nothing and passes.**

**Where each page scrolls, measured 30 Aug at 1440×900** (`barBinding.measure.ts` reports it every run): the SCROLL ROW on Query Centre, Analytics, Contact list, Discover, Manuscripts, Comparable titles and Submission packages; an INTERNAL ZONE on To-do, Calendar and Noteboard, which declare `.tpl-zone, .l-body, .cal-fpbody` and never scroll their frame.

- **⚠️ A PAGE'S OVERFLOW IS REPORTED, NEVER ASSERTED — and this is the correction that came out of §3.** Two measurements required every scrolling page's row to be CURRENTLY overflowing, which is a fact about how much the harness account happens to hold rather than about the page. **It was red on `main` before the masthead rebuild began** — on Manuscripts at `e9f8381b`, and on Submission packages now, whose content is **752px in a 752px scrollport at 900 tall and overflows by 63px at 700**. The page is correct in both cases. **A page HAS one place to scroll and would hand off if it had anything to hand off; whether it does today is the fixture.** This is the same law the old type partition stated in its own prose and then contradicted three assertions later.
- **⚠️ `fill` IS THE LAYOUT, NEVER THE BEHAVIOUR.** `fill` says the row does not scroll and the panes do. It said nothing about the chrome even when there were two chromes — the Tasks family was `fill` and Type A, Query Centre was `fill` and Type B — and conflating them is what produced a page with both a settle and a Hide.
- **⚠️ THE GAP UNDER THE HEADER IS 44px, AND IT IS A VALUE — the half-height rule was offered and REJECTED.** "Half the header's height, rounded" gives 64 from the 128px plate and would move the gap with it for free; **44 is deliberately tighter than half and was chosen over it.** Recorded because that tidiness is exactly what someone later "restores", which would add 20px under every header in the app. Rest geometry: **plate 128 · illustrated mark 88 · title Playfair 38/600 (solo 40) · gap 44**; the working state is untouched at 52/mono-label/mark-gone/`#f2ede7`/35.
- **⚠️ THE GAP IS THE GRID'S ALONE — a page that adds its own top padding defeats the token without contradicting it.** The once-only rule (`.wpg--tools` zeroes the scroll row's copy) was correct and locked, and it guards the wrong half: nothing was watching a page padding the FIRST thing in its scroll row, which is indistinguishable from the gap and simply adds. Measured: Comparable titles rendered **92** against a 70px token (`.ct-pagebody`, +22) and Discover **154** (`.dv-hero`, +84, the ref's value taken from a standalone card with nothing above it), while every page that adds nothing measured exactly 70. **It is not a toolbar thing** — Manuscripts has no toolbar and was clean, To-do has one and was not; the only predictor is whether the page pads its own first row. **A FRAMED box's padding is its own** (`.pkgw-strip`'s 11px sits inside a bordered strip whose edge is at the gap), which is why the check reads the PIXEL — `gapAudit.measure.ts` — rather than the source.
- **⚠️ A CONVERTED PAGE'S `flex: 1; min-height: 0` CHAIN NEEDS A FLEX PARENT, AND `.wpg-scroll` IS A BLOCK.** A page written as a viewport-locked column says `flex: 1; min-height: 0` all the way down; under the workspace grid the parent is the scroll row, so both declarations apply to nothing and the page's own height arithmetic silently stops being load-bearing. **`flex: 1 1 0%` with `min-height: 0` is the worst case: it contributes ZERO to a content-sized container and then has no free space to grow into, so it computes to EXACTLY 0** — with every element inside it mounted, styled and correct. Measured twice: `.tpl-cols` (696px of overflow through a frame whose lock says it never scrolls) and `.f12-body` (Query Centre's whole journey body 0px tall, holding a 167px step stack). **Both hid behind content that happened to size the container**, so a source scan finds neither — Tasks' columns had height of their own, Query Centre's browsing state was propped up by the query list, and only the journey, which hides the list, had nothing left. **The fix is the grid's `fill` prop** (`.wpg--fill > .wpg-scroll { display: flex; flex-direction: column }`), opt-in because on a flowing page a `flex: 1` child would start filling the row instead of flowing past it. **The tell is a page that scrolls where its spec says the panes do** — same fault, milder symptom. Locked by measurement, not source: the acceptance matrix fails any box in a scroll row that measures 0 while holding rendered content.
- **⚠️ A HARNESS THAT SUPPRESSES ANIMATION CANNOT EXERCISE ANY FLOW THAT TEARS DOWN ON `animationend`.** `animation: none` does **not** fire `animationend` — so a takeover whose exit is driven by that event arms its exit class and then never leaves, at any timing, looking exactly like a broken exit. This cost time twice in one session on the same stylesheet: once reporting `animationName: none` on a class whose only job is to run an animation ("the motion is dead" — it was not), and once reporting that Escape did not close the Query Centre journey (it does). **Suppress for static geometry; lift it and WAIT for any test that changes state.** `Queries.tsx` already carried the warning in prose, which did not help — the harness is a different file.
- **⚠️ A `ResizeObserver` ON A SCROLLER SAYS NOTHING WHEN ITS CONTENT GROWS.** It fires when that element's own box changes; `scrollHeight` growing because children arrived inside it is not a resize of the scroller. So anything derived from "is there content below" is computed once at mount and then never again on a page whose data loads asynchronously — **measured: Query Centre reported no bottom fade with 729px beneath the fold while every synchronously-rendered page was correct**, which is the shape that survives a whole pass unnoticed. **Observe the direct CHILDREN as well, and re-sync that list with a `MutationObserver` on `childList`.** Anything derived from `scrollTop` alone is immune — which is why the header state was right and only the fade was wrong.
- **⚠️ AN OVERLAY THAT LIVES INSIDE A RE-RENDERED REGION IS LOST WITH IT — LIFT IT OUT FIRST.** *(24 Sep, the Birds-eye view's lane.)* The nav, the zoom, the edge markers and the crosshair tag all sit OVER the scrolling track, and in the mockup they are children of the thing that rebuilds when the rows do. Every zoom and every filter therefore tore them down and put them back, which is a flicker at best and, where one of them holds a ref or a measurement, a value silently reset on an interaction the reader thinks is about the rows. **They are siblings of the scroller, not children of it**, and the lock is a rendered one: change the zoom, change the grouping, filter, reset — and the controls are still the same elements at the same boxes.
- **⚠️ AND CLOSING IS A RE-RENDER TOO — THE TEARDOWN NEEDS THE SAME LIFT, ACROSS TWO COMMITS.** *(25 Sep, the expanded card.)* The rule above is about a region rebuilding *while it stays on screen*; the same overlays have to leave **before** the thing that hosts them does. An overlay portalled into a card, and any state the card owns that is drawn outside its own subtree, is torn down in the SAME commit as the card when a single handler sets both away — so "first I dismiss the panel, then I close" is a sentence about statement order and not about what the browser paints. **Set the lifts, then defer the host's own close to the next frame**; one synchronous call batches them and the order means nothing.
  - **THE TELL IS A HANDLER THAT READS AS A SEQUENCE AND IS NOT ONE.** `setMenu(null); setFind(""); onClose();` looks like three steps and is one commit. If the point of the first two is that they happen *before* the third, they need a frame between them, and the reason belongs at the value — this is the same family as a compensation written as a `margin` that collapses away: correct arithmetic about a page the browser does not lay out that way.
  - **AND EVERY WAY OUT USES THE ONE EXIT.** A ✕, a backdrop and an Escape key are three call sites for one act; the moment one of them closes by its own route it is a second behaviour, and the one that diverges is always the key handler, because it is registered once with no dependencies and reaches for whatever ref it captured. Hold the exit in a ref the handler reads.
- **⚠️ AN IMAGE PLACED BESIDE MEASURED TEXT TAKES ITS POSITION FROM THE TEXT'S MEASURED BOX, NEVER A CONSTANT.** *(24 Sep, the expanded header's courier.)* The mock draws the head 34px after the title's right edge, and the tidy way to build that is to measure the title once, in one browser, at one width, and write the number down. **A constant is right for the one string, at the one size, in the one font that happened to have loaded when it was measured** — and wrong the moment the type steps at a breakpoint, the copy is edited, or the webfont arrives after the first paint. The component publishes the title's own `getBoundingClientRect().width` as `--qcv-xp-tw` and the sheet reads `calc(<pad> + var(--qcv-xp-tw) + 34px)`.
  - **⚠️ AND IT IS RE-PUBLISHED ON `document.fonts.ready`, WHICH IS THE HALF A SINGLE MEASUREMENT MISSES.** The first read happens in the fallback face; every later one is in Playfair, and the two differ by enough to put the picture through the last word. Publish at layout AND again when the fonts settle, and refuse a zero (`if (w > 0)`) — a width read before layout is not a narrow title, it is no title.
  - **THE TELL IS A NUMBER IN A `left:` THAT NAMES SOMETHING ELSE'S SIZE.** If the value you are about to type is "how wide that other thing is", it belongs to that other thing, and the only honest way to have it is to ask.
- **⚠️ A DRAG SURFACE EXCLUDES EVERY CONTROL DRAWN ON IT.** *(24 Sep, the expanded view's date tier — and it is the mock's own fault, which is why it needs writing down rather than noticing.)* A pan bound to a scroller with `setPointerCapture` swallows the click of every button inside it: the press starts the gesture, the capture takes the pointer away from the control, and the control looks **dead** while its handler is perfectly correct. `onPointerDown` refuses to start on `button, a, input, [role='button']` and on the named regions — the markers, the names column, the card's own controls — and the exclusion list is the drag's whole safety.
  - **⚠️ IT IS MEASURED, NOT READ.** A source lock over the exclusion list proves the list was written; only a rendered gesture proves a press on the zoom pill still zooms. The pair is two gestures: one on the dates, where the track must move with the pointer, and one on a control, where it must not move at all **and** the control must still take the press. A click test cannot see this — the fault only exists for a press that moves.
  - **AND THE RELEASE IS BOUND TWICE** (`pointerup` AND `pointercancel`): a drag that only listens for the first leaves the body unselectable and the cursor stuck at `grabbing` the moment the browser cancels the gesture.
- **⚠️ A POINTER READING BELONGS TO THE BOX IT WAS MEASURED AGAINST, AND CONVERTING IT IS A NAMED OFFSET OR A SILENT LIE.** *(24 Sep, the expanded view's crosshair.)* `scrollLeft + (clientX − scrollerRect.left)` is an x in the SCROLLER's content space; the track begins `NAMES_W` into that content, because the names column is a sticky cell covering the first `NAMES_W` of the viewport. Handing the first straight to a function that reads the second drew the line **298px right of the pointer** and named a day **a fortnight late** — with every declaration correct, every unit test green, and a production build clean.
  - **IT SURVIVES REVIEW BECAUSE BOTH SPACES ARE REAL AND NEITHER IS NAMED.** The two are one subtraction apart and the code reads identically. The lock is a rendered one and it is the cheapest possible: **the crosshair's line lands on the pointer**, within one day of snapping, at every position of a sweep — with the day's width derived from the tier's own week ticks rather than typed.
- **⚠️ `position: sticky` CANNOT ESCAPE AN ANCESTOR THAT CLIPS — THE CLIPPER BECOMES ITS SCROLLPORT.** *(24 Sep, the expanded rows' sentences.)* A label stuck inside a bar carrying `overflow: hidden` sticks to the BAR, which never scrolls, so a bar beginning a thousand pixels off-screen takes its words with it. Measured: ink at **−8360** against a visible edge of **568**. The honest form is to compute the inset — how far the bar's left is behind the track's visible edge — and apply it as a **`margin-left`**, because a margin also takes the width away, which is what turns "the sentence does not fit" into the bar's own ellipsis.
- **⚠️ PLACE A TIME LINE FROM THE ROWS' OWN TRACK, NEVER FROM PADDING ARITHMETIC.** The "today" line, the bars, the crosshair and the date tier are four things that must land on one x, and the only way they cannot disagree is for all four to be placed by the same function from the same origin — `xAt(ext, pxd, ms)`, with the names column added once. Computing any of them as "the container's padding plus the names column plus the scroll offset" reproduces the origin by hand, and a single change to the padding then moves one of the four. **The tell is a number in a `left:` that is not `xAt(…)` plus a named constant.**
- **⚠️ A CONTROL DRAWN OVER SCROLLING CONTENT NEEDS ITS OWN LANE.** A button placed in the same box as the content it floats over is a button the content scrolls under, so it has to win on `z-index` — and then everything else that must be visible there has to win against IT. The Birds-eye view gives the controls a 52px lane of their own above the date tier: they take no part in the scroll, nothing passes beneath them, and the one thing that shares the lane (the crosshair tag) is clamped clear of them by their MEASURED width rather than by a stacking order. **Stacking decides what is on top; it never decides what is legible.**
- **⚠️ CSS GRID AUTO-PLACEMENT NEVER OVERLAPS — two items share a cell only when BOTH are explicitly positioned.** Adding an explicitly-placed overlay (`grid-row: 3; grid-column: 1`) to a grid whose other rows are auto-placed does not stack them: the auto-placed item is pushed into an **implicit new column**, and the grid silently grows sideways. Measured when the scroll hems landed: the header rows read **688px of right margin against another page's 449**, varying with the viewport, on nine of ten pages. **Declare `grid-template-columns` and give every participating row its `grid-column`** — on all of them, not just the newcomer; removing it from one takes that row out of the column.
- **⚠️ AN INTERSECTIONOBSERVER FIRES ONLY ON A *CHANGE* OF INTERSECTION, SO A MISSED EVENT IS PERMANENT.** A sentinel-and-observer is the wrong shape for "is this scroller at the top": if the callback is not delivered — because the sentinel was laid out after the observation began, or resized, or never left the box — nothing ever corrects it, and the state stays wrong for the life of the page. **Derive the state from the value itself**: `scrollTop > 2`, read on scroll, rAF-throttled, written only when it differs, with a `ResizeObserver` re-evaluating it. A derived reading cannot go stale; an event you did not receive can.
  - **⚠️ AND THE COMPANION TRAP: OPENING DEVTOOLS DOCKED SHRINKS THE VIEWPORT AND INFLATES EVERY OVERFLOW READING.** Manuscripts measured **267px** of overflow with the console docked and **42px** undocked — 62px of difference, which is exactly the size of the reclaim being investigated. Every number taken while diagnosing described a page shorter than the one being looked at, and the page appeared to "start working when the console opens". **Undock before measuring, or measure through Playwright.**
- **⚠️ THE SETTLE'S COMPENSATION MUST BE PUBLISHED THROUGH A RENDER, NOT WRITTEN IN THE rAF THAT MEASURES IT — AND THIS IS THE OPPOSITE OF WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE.** *(24 Aug, and it cost a deployed regression.)* The pinned chrome shrinks when it settles and a spacer grows by the same amount so the column's total never changes. Moving that write out of React state and into the `requestAnimationFrame` callback that measures the slab looks strictly better — no render sits between the measurement and the write — and it **broke every small scroll on every scrolling page**. The browser's scroll-anchoring pass does not account for a style set that way, so it takes the uncompensated delta out of `scrollTop` on each frame of the settle, and those deltas sum to the whole settle.
  - **MEASURED on Contact list, same commit, same build, one line different:** `set 10 → held 0 · 20 → 0 · 40 → 0 · 80 → 34` written imperatively, against `10 → 9 · 20 → 19 · 40 → 39 · 80 → 79` through state. The reader loses ~46px of position on any scroll shorter than the settle.
  - **THE GENERAL FORM: "fewer frames between measurement and write" is not the property that matters. What matters is that the compensation lands somewhere the browser's anchoring pass can see it.** Do not "optimise" this back into the rAF callback; it reads as an improvement and costs the reader their place.
  - **AND IT IS INVISIBLE TO EVERY LOCK THAT SCROLLS BY A LOT.** The measurement harness drives 300–600px, where 46px of loss is noise; the fault only shows on a scroll of the same order as the settle. A small-scroll hold check is the one that catches it, and it is what proved the mechanism both ways round.
- **⚠️ A PAGE THAT OVERFLOWS BY LESS THAN ITS CHROME IS TALL CANNOT SETTLE STABLY.** *(Same pack, found while building the withdrawn retract; recorded because the hazard is real whether or not anything acts on it.)* Settling reclaims height, and on a page whose scroller barely overflows that reclaim destroys the scroll the settled state is derived from. Measured: Noteboard's zone overflows by **47px** at 1440 against a 166px chrome — max scroll went 47 → 0, `scrollTop` clamped, the page un-settled, and the chrome grew back, **37 times on one downward pass**. Anything that increases what the chrome sheds must ask first whether the page can afford it, using the RESTING overflow — gating on the current figure re-creates the oscillation one level up, because settling is what makes the figure fall.
- **⚠️ THE `background` SHORTHAND RESETS `background-color` TO TRANSPARENT — so a gradient that fails to paint leaves NOTHING.** *(24 Aug, the masthead's parchment wash, and it is the fifth entry in a sequence about a see-through header.)* `background: linear-gradient(…)` is not "add an image"; it is the shorthand, and it sets every background longhand including the colour. The gradient itself then becomes the *only* fill: drop it — an unparsed stop, a token that resolves to nothing, a `color-mix()` an engine declines, a future edit that mistypes one hex — and the element is see-through, silently, with the rule still in the sheet looking correct.
  - **THE SHAPE: declare the colour and the image SEPARATELY**, so the worst case is a flat fill rather than a transparent one. `background-color: var(--mast-wash-bottom); background-image: linear-gradient(180deg, var(--mast-wash-top), var(--mast-wash-bottom));` — and make the colour the stop that meets the element's closing edge, so the fallback matches the wash where the hairline lands.
  - **⚠️ IT IS INVISIBLE TO EVERY CHECK THAT READS `backgroundColor`.** A lock asserting the fill is opaque by reading `getComputedStyle(el).backgroundColor` reports `rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)` for a *correctly* painted gradient — so the honest assertion covers the colour **and** every `rgb()` stop inside `background-image`. That distinction had to be added the day the flat fill became a wash, and asserting only the first would have been a lock that failed on a correct page while missing the real fault.
- **⚠️ A LOCK THAT COMPARES A THING WITH ITSELF SURVIVES CHANGES TO THE THING; ONE THAT COMPARES IT TO AN EXPECTED VALUE DOES NOT.** *(24 Aug — noticed because the wash needed exactly one lock amended and the harder one needed none.)* The pixel sweep proving nothing reads through the pinned chrome photographs the slab over two different backdrops **in the same posture** and requires the two images identical. When the flat fill became a gradient it needed **no change at all** — what the chrome is filled with never enters the claim. The sibling lock, which compared the computed fill against one expected colour, broke immediately and correctly.
  - **THE GENERAL FORM:** where a property must be *invariant*, assert the invariance directly rather than pinning the value the invariance currently produces. Pinning the value is a lock that goes red on every legitimate redesign — which trains the next person to rebaseline it without reading it — and, worse, the obvious loosening ("assert it is *not* the content's colour") passes vacuously.
- **⚠️ WHEN A SUITE REPORTS A FAILURE, GREP FOR THE ASSERTION'S TEXT TO FIND WHICH FILE OWNS IT — DO NOT ASSUME FROM THE NAME.** *(24 Aug, and it produced a confident wrong conclusion in two runs.)* A ten-file run went red on *"something between the scroll row and the content declares its own width"*. That sentence sounds like the content-geometry lock and lives in `matrix.measure.ts`. Re-running `contentGeometry.measure.ts` twice — once with the change, once without — produced **two clean passes and a conclusion that the failure was order-dependent state leakage**, which was about to be written up as a finding. The file simply does not contain the case.
  - **THE COST IS THE SHAPE, NOT THE MINUTES:** both runs were real, both readings were true, and both described a subject that was never in question — the same fault as measuring a harness instead of the app, or the deployed site instead of your bundle. **A green from the wrong file is indistinguishable from a green from the right one.**
  - **THE MECHANICAL STEP IS ONE COMMAND:** `grep -rln "<a distinctive phrase from the failure>" tests/e2e/`. Do it before re-running anything, and quote the file it names in the report.
  - **AND THE SECOND EXPERIMENT IS THE ONE THAT SETTLES AUTHORSHIP:** run the owning file against the baseline WITHOUT your change. Only that separates "my change did this" from "this was already red on main" — and here it proved the reds belonged to another stream's commit, which is a thing to report rather than to fix or to rebaseline.
- **⚠️ UNDER A DISPLAY-TOGGLING SHELL, A LOCATOR'S `.first()` ROUTINELY MATCHES A PAGE THAT IS NOT ON SCREEN.** *(24 Aug; the sibling of the portal-outliving-its-page note above, and it costs a timeout rather than a wrong reading.)* The workspace keeps every page MOUNTED and toggles `display`, and **three pages share `tpl-wpg`** — so `page.locator(".wpg.tpl-wpg .wpg-chrome").first()` resolves to a hidden grid and Playwright waits the full test timeout for it to become "stable", reporting *"element is not visible"* about a page that is perfectly visible. **Select the visible grid by measuring it** — `[...document.querySelectorAll(sel)].find(e => e.getBoundingClientRect().height > 0)` — then tag it and target the tag. Every lock in `tests/e2e/` that reads a `.wpg` already does this; a new probe is where the fault reappears.
  - **AND THE CENSUS IS SPLICED, NEVER RETYPED.** Writing the ten pages' route/class list by hand for a new probe produced **two wrong classes out of three** in one sitting (`aglist` for `agl-wpg`, `anl-wpg` for `qa-wpg`) — the same family as a test handing a function an input its callers cannot produce. Take the list from the lock that already has it.
- **⚠️ A PAGE'S RULE AND A COMPONENT'S RULE AT EQUAL SPECIFICITY: THE PAGE WINS, BECAUSE ITS SHEET IS LATER IN THE BUNDLE.** `.wpg--working > .wpg-scroll { padding-bottom: … }` is 0-2-0 and so is `.aglist .wpg-scroll { padding-bottom: 48px }` — so three pages silently discarded the grid's scroll-invariance padding, and *only* those three measured max scroll falling by 62 while the other six held. Nothing errors; the cascade is working as specified. **When a component and a page both need to contribute to one property, each contributes a TOKEN and the property is a `calc()` sum** (`padding-bottom: calc(var(--wpg-foot, 0px) + var(--wpg-reclaim-pad, 0px))`). Raising specificity is the wrong fix — it makes one contribution *replace* the other, which is the same bug pointing the other way.
- **⚠️ A GROUPED CSS RULE MAKES FIRST-MATCH SLICING AMBIGUOUS — declare per selector wherever a lock reads the file.** A stylesheet lock that finds its rule with `indexOf(".x {")` reads whichever block comes FIRST, so adding `.x, .y, .z { box-sizing: border-box }` above `.x`'s real rule silently repoints every assertion at a one-property stub. This has now caught `workspacePageGrid.css` **twice**: once as a genuine duplicate (`.wpg-scroll` declared twice, the lock reading the wrong half) and once inside the very commit that was fixing it — a grouped `box-sizing` rule added for three rows made `.wpg-scroll` ambiguous and the viewport lock reported *"row 3 stopped scrolling"* about a row that scrolls fine. **Two fixes, and prefer the first:** fold the shared property into each selector's own existing rule, so the file keeps one rule per selector (which `workspacePageGrid.css` states as its own invariant); or, where a group is genuinely right, make the helper join EVERY block for the selector, as `oneScreenTasks.test.tsx`'s `rule()` does. The failure is silent in one direction and a false alarm in the other, which is what makes it expensive both times.
- **⚠️ A BOUNDED-SLICE LOCK ASSERTS THAT BOTH ANCHORS EXIST BEFORE SLICING.** *(16 Aug — the same family as the source-string, prefix-match and off-screen-probe cases, and the one that hides longest.)* `indexOf` returns **`-1`** for a marker that is not there, and `slice(-1)` is read as *"one character from the end"* — so `src.slice(src.indexOf("function a"), src.indexOf("function b"))` **silently widens to the whole rest of the file** the day `function b` is renamed. Every assertion over it then covers code it was never meant to see, and it keeps passing until something downstream happens to contain the forbidden string. **The lock does not go red, it goes vague.** The worked example: the board's completion lock sliced `performBoardPlan` → `renderBoard`, `renderBoard` had been deleted, and *every function below `performBoardPlan`* was inside `not.toContain("done: true")` — which then fired on a correct new function and, worse, had been passing for the wrong reason for however long. **Use `src/test/sliceBetween.ts`** (`sliceBetween(src, from, to)` / `sliceFrom(src, from, len)`), which fails loudly naming the missing anchor. ⚠️ **THE SWEEP THAT INTRODUCED IT FOUND NINE MORE, ALL SILENT** — slices anchored on `renderFilterSection`, `renderFilterChips`, `renderTools`, `renderUserCard`, `ShellRail`/`ShellSide` and a `<header>` that `TasksPageLayout` does not have, every one of them reading an entire file. **Two were assigned and never read at all**, so they had asserted nothing since the day they were written. ⚠️ **AND THE FIX IS USUALLY NOT A NEW ANCHOR** — when the anchor is gone the SUBJECT is usually gone, so the honest replacement states the retirement (`expect(page).not.toContain("function renderFilterSection")`) and asserts the rule over the whole file, which is stronger than the bounded version ever was.
- **⚠️ A SLICE IS BOUNDED BY TWO ANCHORS THAT CANNOT NEST — NEVER BY A CLOSING-TAG PATTERN.** *(27 Aug. The sibling of the bounded-slice rule below, and worse, because it fails in the direction that reads as a pass.)* A rendered-HTML lock sliced `/<div class="msp-recstats">([\s\S]*?)<\/div>\s*<\/div>/` to prove a control sat inside that group. `</div>` is not a delimiter — it matches the first NESTED close, so the slice ended before the very element under test and the assertion reported the absence it was written to forbid. **The failure names the right thing and blames the right file; only the boundary is wrong**, which is why it survives review. Bound on two anchors that cannot appear inside each other — a distinctive opening class and the opening class of the NEXT sibling — or assert over `textContent` rather than markup. The same applies to any regex bound: `\*/` in a comment, a closing brace in CSS, a closing quote in a template.
- **String-rendering specs — ANCHOR BEFORE YOU SLICE.** Component specs render through `renderToStaticMarkup` and assert against the HTML string (there is no jsdom in this repo — `vitest.config.ts` is `environment: 'node'`, and neither jsdom nor testing-library is installed). Whenever a spec **slices, splits or extracts** on a class or marker, assert that the marker exists FIRST — one `expect` per slice. A missing marker fails in one of two quiet ways: extracting yields `""` (`?? ""` / `?? []`) and every `.not.toContain` on an empty string passes; or `split(marker)[0]` yields the whole rest of the document, and whether the test notices depends on what happens to sit further down the page. Both go green while testing nothing. Prefer whole-string `toContain`/`toMatch` where it will do the job — `shellV2Smoke.test.tsx` never slices and is immune by construction. Where a slice is genuinely needed, restate its anchor inside each `it` that consumes it rather than relying on a `const` in the enclosing `describe`. (Audit + the failure it came from: `reports/string-spec-slice-audit.md`.)

## Multi-stream protocol (⚠️ SUPERSEDED 8 Jul by "Working discipline" above — kept for history; the shared-checkout model is retired in favour of one-session-per-worktree)
- **Identity gate:** any cross-session note (resume/sync/follow-up prompts) starts with self-identification from the session's OWN history; a note that reaches the wrong stream gets its facts verified, not obeyed — they may be stale (a "tsc is red" another session already fixed; a "pending deploy" that landed an hour ago).
- **Resume = re-read:** after any parking, in-context copies of shared files are stale by definition — re-read from disk before editing. Never edit another stream's uncommitted WIP (live lost-update risk); leave a handover note (BUILD-REPORT/CLAUDE.md/commit message) instead.
- **Commits:** explicit-path staging + `git commit --only -- <paths>` (race-proof against the shared index); `git diff --cached --name-only` must list only your stream's paths. No `git stash` / `checkout --` / `restore` / `clean` on anything, ever. Gates (tsc + production build + full Vitest) green before every commit — with `set -o pipefail` (piping to tail otherwise masks a red tsc, which also emits JS despite errors). If a gate is red from ANOTHER stream's untracked WIP, verify in an isolated HEAD worktree + only your files.
- **Reports:** per-stream report files (the `LOCATION_BUILD_REPORT.md` / `DISCOVER_PHASE1_REPORT.md` pattern). `BUILD-REPORT.md` is the legacy shared file — append-only, and a run note rides the SAME commit as its code (no floating hunks left in shared tracked files).
- **Test counts move several times a day across streams** (523 → 538 → 542 within 5 Jul alone) — record the suite figure fresh at commit time; never copy it from an earlier note or chat.
